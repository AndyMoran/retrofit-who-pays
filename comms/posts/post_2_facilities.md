Three things for anyone writing a retrofit spec this autumn.

One: insulation can cause the damp it's meant to prevent. Solid Victorian walls need to manage moisture. Badly specified or badly fitted wall insulation, internal especially, can trap it inside the wall, and mould follows. Whole-house work to PAS 2035, with ventilation designed in from the start, isn't paperwork. It's what makes the damp argument for retrofit true.

Two: check early whether external insulation is even allowed. It's the better option for moisture and for summer comfort, but conservation areas often bar it on street-facing terraces, taller blocks face post-Grenfell fire rules on external wall systems, and blocks with Right to Buy leaseholders need their consent. Find out at survey stage, because the fallback (internal insulation) changes the cost, the disruption and the moisture risk.

Three: from 30 November 2026, excess heat becomes a legal hazard in social housing on the same day as excess cold. Insulation keeps summer heat in too. Shading for glazing and a night-time purge-ventilation plan belong in the spec, especially for top-floor and south-facing flats.

And the order: top up the loft first, because it's cheap and quick. If the home has storage heaters, they'll be even more oversized once the walls are done, so heater controls are worth more after insulation, not less.
