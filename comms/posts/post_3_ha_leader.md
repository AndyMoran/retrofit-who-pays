If you're a housing association, insulation won't pay you back on bills. Your tenants get every penny of that. Here's what it does pay you back in.

Liability: from 30 November 2026, excess cold (and excess heat) become Awaab's Law hazards with statutory response clocks.
Damp: unaffordable heat leads to under-heating, which leads to mould. 38% of housing association tenants report condensation, damp or mould. One average dry-rot repair is about £4,600, roughly the landlord's net cost of retrofitting a Victorian terrace after the Warm Homes grant.
Carbon: no law makes you reach net zero, but your tenants' heating counts in your own reported emissions (Scope 3), and lenders ask about it. Insulation beyond the loft cuts 240–600 tonnes a year per 1,000 homes, at a net gain per tonne.
Compliance: EPC C by 1 April 2030 on any ONE of three routes (fabric, smart readiness, heating system). Insulation is the only one that cuts heat demand.

Two corrections to August. The Boiler Upgrade Scheme isn't open to social housing providers, for any heat pump. And fabric isn't the only compliance route: batteries, storage heaters and smart controls are named under smart readiness, though the thresholds aren't out yet.

Do the loft first, because it's cheap. Then put insulation and any heat pump in ONE Wave 3 application while the home is still rated D to G, and spend it by 31 March 2028.

(To be upfront: the link from EPC rating to balance-sheet valuation is my own inference, not a sourced valuation method.)
